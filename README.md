# Divergent genetic insecticide resistance in Gambian malaria vector: novel COE under selection in coastal *Anopheles arabiensis*

Welcome to the GitHub repository accompanying our manuscript:

> **Title:** Divergent genetic insecticide resistance in Gambian malaria vector: novel COE under selection in coastal *Anopheles arabiensis*


> **Authors:** ​​Fatoumata Seck, Jane N Mwangi, Chris S Clarkson, Saihou Cham, Saadbouh Faal, Musa Jawara, Ebrima Jatta, Kelly L. Bennett, Umberto D’Alessandro, Alfred A Ngwa, Alistair Miles, Kevin Opondo and Benoit S Assogba


> **Corresponding Author:** Benoit S Assogba, Sessinou-Benoit.Assogba@lshtm.ac.uk

This repository contains information on extracting the data, analysis scripts, and figures associated with our study exploring the emergence and spread of insecticide resistance in *Anopheles arabiensis* across The Gambia using whole-genome sequencing.

### Abstract
Recent genomic data reveal that *Anopheles arabiensis* is becoming the dominant malaria vector in The Gambia, accompanied by increased insecticide resistance. Through whole-genome sequencing and population genomics analyses of 314 *An. arabiensis* individuals from seven administrative regions, we identified:

- Strong geographic genetic structure between coastal and inland populations
  
- Widespread kdr mutations (L995F/L995S) in coastal populations
  
- Amplified metabolic resistance genes (e.g., *Cyp6aa/p*, *Cyp9k1*)
  
- Novel selection at a carboxylesterase gene cluster (Coeae) in coastal populations

Our findings highlight a localised hotspot of resistance evolution, with implications for vector control strategies in The Gambia.
